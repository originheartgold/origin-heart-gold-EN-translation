# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People playing the Origin HeartGold English patch, usually mid-game with the DS or an emulator in front of them and the site open on a phone. They come with one question ("where is Steven?", "can I still get Bruno?", "what does this quest need?"), want the answer in seconds, and go back to playing. A smaller group reads a chapter ahead of time on a laptop. Translators and maintainers use the same pages to check facts.

## Product Purpose

The site ships the patch and is the companion reference for the game: a chapter-by-chapter walkthrough with every side quest and missable event, plus the Pokédex, locations, items, trainers and tools (team builder, damage calculator, save editor). Success is a player who never gets stuck or misses a one-chance event without knowing it, and who can tick off what they've done and find what's next without scrolling.

## Brand Personality

Knowledgeable, calm, exact. The voice of a patient friend who has finished the game three times: plain words, no hype, no game-magazine excitement. Warm through the HeartGold gold accent and the paper-and-ink palette, never through decoration.

## Anti-references

- Boxed dashboard UI: bordered cards, cards inside cards, chips and badges on everything.
- A wiki wall of text: long unbroken prose with no way to skim, jump or tick things off.
- The default Astro Starlight docs look, with nothing that says this is a game companion.
- Gamified apps: progress bars, percentages, XP-style counters, confetti.

## Design Principles

- One question, one answer: every screen is built around the thing a player needs right now, with everything else a tap away.
- Flat and sequential: the walkthrough is a sequence, so it reads as a sequence (steps in order, quests under the step that opens them), not as a grid of boxes.
- Ticks are the only state: a quest is done or not. No scores, no completion metrics.
- Same vocabulary everywhere: the Done tick, the tag pills and the step rows look the same on every page.
- Phone first, desktop unchanged: phone layouts are rethought, not shrunk; the wide layout keeps working as it does.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Touch targets at least 44 px. Every control works with keyboard and screen readers (real buttons, native dialogs, labelled checkboxes). Motion is 150–250 ms and switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Both themes keep body text at 4.5:1 or better. Some players use the site next to a running game, so nothing should need a steady hand or a hover.
